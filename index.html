Failing Siding Isn’t Just an Eyesore — It’s an Opening

You’ve noticed it for a while. Cracked panels. Warping along the south face. Paint that’s peeling faster than it should be, or a section that sounds hollow when you knock on it. Maybe you’ve spotted the staining inside near an exterior wall and started wondering if those two things are connected.

They are.

Siding doesn’t fail gracefully. When the exterior envelope of a house starts to go, water finds its way behind the panels — slowly at first, then increasingly. The moisture barrier that was supposed to stop it is often already compromised by the time visible damage shows up outside. By then, you’re not just replacing siding. You’re replacing siding, moisture barrier, and potentially the sheathing or framing behind it.

Baton Rouge homeowners who wait on deteriorating siding aren’t saving money. They’re letting a $10,000 job become a $25,000 job, one rainstorm at a time.

What Deteriorating Siding Is Doing Right Now

The damage from failing siding compounds invisibly between inspections:

  • Water intrusion: Even a small gap or crack in siding creates a path for water to get behind the panels. Louisiana’s extreme humidity, Gulf Coast storms, and hurricane season make siding integrity a genuine structural issue — failing siding lets moisture into wall cavities fast, leading to mold and rot within one wet season.
  • Mold and rot: Once moisture establishes behind a wall, mold and wood rot follow within months. Remediation is expensive — and it’s completely hidden from the outside. You won’t know it’s there until it’s extensive.
  • Energy loss: Failing siding creates air infiltration paths. In Baton Rouge’s climate, that means you’re heating or cooling the outside. Homeowners with deteriorating siding spend noticeably more on energy each month, though the siding rarely gets the blame.
  • Resale hit: Real estate agents in Baton Rouge know that visibly worn or damaged siding triggers buyer skepticism about the whole house. It’s one of the fastest ways to drop your listing price or lose a qualified buyer — particularly if a home inspection flags it.
  • HOA and code exposure: Some Baton Rouge HOAs issue notices on visibly deteriorating siding. If you’re past that point and haven’t addressed it, the notice is likely coming.

A siding project isn’t a luxury upgrade. For most Baton Rouge homes showing these signs, it’s deferred maintenance that’s actively getting more expensive.

What Does Siding Replacement Cost in Baton Rouge?

Most contractors won’t post this. Here’s what you’ll actually pay.

By Material Type

  • Vinyl siding: $5,000–$14,000 for an average Baton Rouge home. Most affordable upfront, good performance when properly installed. 20–30 year lifespan with minimal maintenance.
  • Fiber cement (James Hardie): $9,000–$20,000. Better performance in Louisiana’s humidity and storm conditions. Highly resistant to moisture, impact, and temperature cycling. 30–50 year lifespan when maintained. Most popular upgrade choice.
  • Wood / cedar: $12,000–$30,000. Premium aesthetics. Requires the most ongoing maintenance. Best suited for specific architectural styles.

Key Cost Drivers

  • Home size: Measured in sq ft of exterior surface, not floor plan. A two-story 2,000 sq ft home can have 2,500–3,500 sq ft of exterior wall surface.
  • Existing siding removal: $1,000–$3,000 depending on layers and condition.
  • Moisture barrier and sheathing condition: If the housewrap or sheathing behind old siding is degraded, replacement is necessary and not optional. We assess this during the estimate and price it separately so you’re never surprised.
  • Trim and detail complexity: Deep eaves, dormers, complex window surrounds, and soffit work all add time and cost.

What’s NOT in these numbers: padding for uncertainty. We assess the full scope before quoting, not after we start.

Why Baton Rouge’s Climate Changes How Siding Should Be Installed

Louisiana’s extreme humidity, Gulf Coast storms, and hurricane season make siding integrity a genuine structural issue — failing siding lets moisture into wall cavities fast, leading to mold and rot within one wet season. This creates failure conditions that many national installation guides underestimate:

  • Moisture cycling: Repeated wet-dry cycles from rain and humidity cause cheaper siding materials and improperly installed housewrap to degrade faster. We use moisture barriers rated for Baton Rouge’s precipitation patterns, not contractor-grade minimum-spec wrap.
  • Thermal expansion: Baton Rouge’s temperature range means exterior materials expand and contract significantly through the year. Vinyl installed too tight will buckle in summer heat. We follow manufacturer specifications for expansion gaps.
  • Sun exposure: South and west-facing walls take the most UV punishment. Material selection and color choice affect how fast fading and degradation occur. We flag this during the estimate.
  • Wind-driven rain: In Baton Rouge, horizontal rain events create water pressure that finds any improperly sealed penetration. Electrical boxes, hose bibs, window flanges — every penetration gets properly flashed and sealed on every job.

We’ve replaced siding across Baton Rouge, Zachary, Central, Denham Springs, and surrounding East Baton Rouge Parish communities. The conditions we’ve described aren’t theoretical — they’re what we find when we pull damaged siding off homes every week.

Frequently Asked Questions

Get answers to common siding installation questions from Baton Rouge homeowners.

How do I know which material is right for my home?
The right choice depends on your climate exposure, your home’s architectural style, your budget, and how much maintenance you want long-term. During the estimate, we walk through the pros and cons of each option for your specific situation. Most Baton Rouge homeowners end up on vinyl or fiber cement for a reason — we’ll explain why on the visit.
How disruptive is the installation process?
You’ll hear noise during working hours, but your home stays livable throughout. We work in sections so no wall is ever fully exposed at end-of-day. Most full-house jobs take 3–7 days depending on size and complexity.
Can you just repair the damaged section instead of doing the whole house?
Sometimes yes — if the damage is truly isolated and the rest of the siding is in good condition. More often, patching one section highlights the age of the rest, and the cost difference between a partial and full job is smaller than expected. We’ll give you an honest assessment of both options.
What if there’s damage behind the siding I don’t know about?
This is the most important question in any siding project. Our estimate process specifically looks for moisture intrusion evidence before we quote. If we find unexpected damage after we start, we stop, show you, and get your approval on any additional scope before continuing. You are never surprised by a bill for work you didn’t see coming.
Do you pull permits?
Yes, when Baton Rouge requires them. Permit-pulling protects you — it means the work gets inspected and creates documentation that the installation was done to code. Any contractor who tries to skip permits to “speed things up” is saving themselves hassle, not you.
Do you handle insurance claims for storm damage?
Yes — Louisiana homeowners policies often cover storm-damaged siding. We document damage thoroughly, work with adjusters, and handle all required repairs. We’ll let you know upfront whether your damage is likely to qualify.
How much does siding installation cost in Baton Rouge?
Vinyl siding runs $5–$12/sq ft installed; fiber cement (HardiePlank) $8–$16/sq ft; wood $12–$20/sq ft. A typical 1,500–2,000 sq ft Baton Rouge home costs $8,000–$20,000 fully sided. Larger homes or those needing moisture barrier replacement run higher. Written line-item quotes provided at every estimate.
What warranties come with siding installation?
James Hardie fiber cement carries a 30-year limited warranty; most vinyl siding manufacturers offer lifetime limited warranties. Our workmanship warranty covers installation defects for a minimum of 5 years. All warranty documentation is provided at project completion.
What happens if you find damage behind the siding during installation?
We stop, photograph the damage, and show you before continuing. You receive a revised quote for any additional scope and must approve it in writing before work proceeds. In Baton Rouge’s climate, moisture barrier and sheathing damage is common — especially on south-facing walls. We pre-assess for this during the estimate to minimize mid-job surprises.
How does Louisiana’s humidity affect siding material choice?
Baton Rouge averages 70–100 inches of rain annually and summer humidity routinely exceeds 90%. Wood siding requires repainting every 3–5 years in this climate. Vinyl can warp in intense heat. Fiber cement holds paint 10–15 years between repaints, making it the most cost-effective long-term choice for most Louisiana homes.
What areas do you serve in greater Baton Rouge?
We serve East and West Baton Rouge, Ascension, and Livingston parishes — including Gonzales, Prairieville, Zachary, Baker, Port Allen, Walker, Denham Springs, Sorrento, and Plaquemine. Travel charges may apply for jobs more than 45 minutes from Baton Rouge; we confirm this upfront.
